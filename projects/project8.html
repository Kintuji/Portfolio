Overview

Graveyard Mansion is a top-down psychological survival horror game combining exploration, puzzles, and resource management across a multi-level mansion — from the graveyard entrance through numbered floors, puzzle rooms, and character-specific story levels. I joined the project as an intern focused on quality and tooling rather than new features: making the existing game more stable, and making the team faster at finding out when it wasn't.

This is a professional, team project owned by Aden Interactive — the write-up below covers what I specifically built and contributed, not the game as a whole.

The Problem

A growing codebase, and testing that cost too much time

Graveyard Mansion is a large game — the level list alone spans a graveyard, a mansion, several character-specific story levels, and multiple mansion floors with their own puzzle rooms. As the codebase grew, structure and reliability started slipping, and game-breaking edge cases were surfacing during testing that took real time to track down and reproduce.

The bigger cost, though, was iteration speed: testing a specific dialogue line or a bug three floors into the mansion meant playing through the game from the start every time. On a project this size, that's not a minor inconvenience — it's QA and design work that never gets done because it's too slow to get to the part that matters.

My Approach

Fix the code, then fix how fast we can test it

I split the work into two halves. First, refactoring: going through the core codebase to improve its structure and maintainability, so bugs were easier to isolate and fixes were less likely to break something else. Alongside that, I led QA — systematically identifying, reproducing, and resolving critical gameplay and system-level bugs rather than fixing whatever surfaced first, which is what actually moved overall stability.

Second, and the part I'm most proud of: I built a runtime debug tool that runs inside the game itself. It lets anyone on the team travel directly to any scene or level and add inventory items on demand, instead of playing through the game from the start to reach the moment or the item they need to test.

The dialogue controls specifically let a tester select and trigger any dialogue on demand, then choose how it ends — and each ending state means something different for testing. Skip moves past the current line without leaving the conversation. Finish completes the whole dialogue and marks it as read/checked, the same as if the player had gone through it naturally. Close exits without finishing, leaving that dialogue in the state as if it had never been triggered at all. That distinction mattered for QA: it let the team test both what happens when a conversation is actually completed and what happens if a player backs out of one mid-way, without needing to play through it twice to check both paths.

Result

A more stable game, and a team that could test it faster

The refactor and QA pass measurably improved game stability — fewer edge cases and game-breaking issues made it into builds. The debug tool changed how the team tested day to day: instead of every bug report requiring a full replay to reach a scene or collect a specific item, anyone could travel straight there and reproduce it in seconds, which is what actually made the QA process sustainable on a project this size.

My Contributions

  • Refactored core systems in a growing, multi-level codebase (graveyard, mansion floors, puzzle rooms, character story levels) to make bugs easier to isolate and fixes less likely to break something else
  • Led QA by systematically reproducing critical gameplay and system-level bugs back to their root cause, instead of patching whatever symptom surfaced first
  • Cut down game-breaking edge cases making it into builds by tracing those root causes rather than working around individual reports
  • Built an in-game debug tool letting any tester travel directly to any level, drive dialogue through Skip/Finish/Close states, and spawn inventory items on demand — turning a full replay into a seconds-long reproduction step

Technical Focus

Codebase refactoring, QA methodology, and in-editor/runtime tooling — building a debug panel that exposes scene loading, dialogue state, and inventory/resources for fast, repeatable testing.
