Level of Dimensions

A solo-developed horror puzzle game where you wake up shrunk to toy-size inside a huge, dark house — and a single flashlight is your only key, your only weapon, and your only way to see the world.

Unity / C# Solo Developer Horror / Puzzle

How to Play

You're dreaming, but you don't know it — shrunk down Toy Story-small inside a massive house, one room per level. Mannequins patrol the dark, watching a 45° cone ahead of them; get seen and they'll come for you. Your flashlight is the answer to almost everything: hit a patrolling mannequin with it and it's blinded, but keep it on too long and you run out of energy. In Level 1, you're hunting five hidden keys to open the way out — one of them only appears if you notice a painting on the wall worth aiming your light at, revealing a secret door. Grab the last key and an NPC notices — a portal opens, and you're through to Level 2.

Level 2 changes the rules: the same flashlight now reveals platforms that are otherwise invisible, so crossing a gap means sweeping your light across the dark to find out where to jump. You've also picked up a ranged weapon by now — enemies shoot back, and you have three lives to lose before it's over. Find the keys, read what's scattered around each level, and find the last portal to finish the game. Turns out it was all a dream.

Overview

Level of Dimensions is a two-level horror puzzle game I built entirely solo — design, programming, and all. Each level is a single room of a dream-house, scaled so the player feels tiny against the space around them. The whole game is built around one idea: your flashlight isn't just a light source, it's the mechanic the entire puzzle and stealth loop runs on.

My Contributions

Since this was a solo project, everything below — design, AI, mechanics, atmosphere, and optimisation — was mine to build.

  • Patrol AI with real vision detection — built the mannequin enemies as a state machine patrolling fixed routes, with a 45° forward vision cone rather than omniscient player-tracking, so staying out of sight is an actual spatial problem, not luck.
  • One mechanic, two puzzle types — designed the flashlight as a single energy-limited system reused for two different purposes across the two levels: in Level 1 it blinds and stuns a mannequin caught in the beam, and in Level 2 it reveals platforms that are otherwise invisible, turning traversal into a search-and-light puzzle.
  • Key & objective system — built the five-key progression gate for Level 1, including a hidden key that only unlocks once the player notices and aims their light at a specific painting to open a secret door.
  • Level transition logic — scripted the reaction to collecting the final key: an NPC registers the theft and a portal spawns, moving the player into the next level without a manual trigger or loading screen.
  • Combat & player mechanics (Level 2) — added a ranged projectile weapon and enemies that shoot back, on top of core movement (walking, jumping) and a three-life health system that takes damage from enemy hits.
  • Atmosphere & performance — used post-processing and flickering lights to build the horror tone, and implemented occlusion culling so the game only renders what the camera can actually see, keeping a dark, effects-heavy scene running smoothly.

Technical Focus

State-machine patrol AI with vision-cone detection, a single resource-managed mechanic (the flashlight) driving two distinct puzzle systems, ranged combat, and rendering optimisation through occlusion culling and post-processing.
