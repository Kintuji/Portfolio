Jester

A horror maze game built for Scream Game Jam 2025 — find the exit code while a clown named Jester patrols the labyrinth, hunting by sight.

Unity / C# AI Programmer Windows Released
Play on Itch.io

How to Play

Numbers are scattered across the walls and circus balls of the maze. Find them, work out the right order, and use that code to unlock the exit — before Jester finds you. Valves around the map drain the water to keep your movement speed up. Jester always knows roughly where you are, so staying hidden matters as much as solving the puzzle.

Overview

Jester is a survival-horror maze game made in Unity for Scream Game Jam 2025, now in continued development. The whole game hinges on one thing working well: Jester himself needs to feel like a real threat that's actually watching for you, not a scripted timer that jumpscares you at a fixed point.

My Contributions

As AI programmer, I built Jester end-to-end: his patrol, his detection, his reactions, and the moment he catches you.

  • Patrol & checkpoints — Jester walks a set route between checkpoints around the maze rather than standing still or following a fixed script, so his position is never fully predictable.
  • Vision-based detection — Jester has an actual field of vision. He only reacts to the player when he can see them, rather than knowing the player's position unconditionally, which is what makes sneaking past him a real option instead of impossible.
  • FSM with randomised behaviour — once Jester spots the player, an FSM picks between three reactions — flee, sneak, or attack — rather than always doing the same thing. That randomisation is what keeps a clown enemy unsettling instead of predictable: you can't learn "he always does X" and counter it.
  • Jumpscare on capture — when Jester catches the player, it triggers a dedicated jumpscare sequence rather than just ending the run silently.
  • Animation & sound integration — all of Jester's animations and sound cues are wired up and working across his patrol, detection, and attack states.

Technical Focus

Finite state machines, vision/line-of-sight detection, randomised state selection for unpredictable enemy behaviour, jumpscare sequencing, and animation/audio integration for an AI-driven horror encounter.
